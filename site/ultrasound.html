From masks to measurements

Segmentation models mark muscle boundaries (aponeuroses) and fibre bundles (fascicles). Geometric fits turn those masks into measurements, while ruler ticks convert pixels to millimetres.

Pennation angleAngle between a fibre bundle and the deep muscle boundary.
Fascicle lengthLength along the fibre between the two muscle boundaries.

The UMUD task predicts these quantities for 309 test images. Its score averages absolute errors divided by 6 degrees, 12 mm and 3 mm, respectively. Lower is better.

Models and angle fitting

The first pipeline used two ResNet-34 U-Nets. Correcting ruler scales, selecting the muscle boundaries and retaining fainter fascicle fragments brought its score to 0.45615 in July.

The November experiments followed a public geometry and texture method. It combines measurements from six pretrained model pairs, estimates fibre angle from image texture, and fits fascicle angle against depth within the muscle.

Robust fitting (Huber reweighting) reduced the influence of isolated angle outliers. The ensemble weights stayed the same.

Local checks and competition results

A 35-image expert benchmark exposed measurement bugs, but several changes it preferred worsened competition results. One fascicle-length rule moved the public score from 0.47473 to 0.49983.

The original local scorer supplied the correct ruler scale and adjusted mean fascicle length to the labels. This concealed calibration errors. Removing the adjustment still left a mismatch between the small benchmark and the competition images.

The test images also include new devices, muscles and related frames. Image grouping and the choice of validation examples matter when comparing models.
